# O.C.G.A. § 16-11-90 — Possession of an Illegal Weapon

## Charge Information

> **Felony**  
> **Class:** Class A  
> **Fine:** $40,000  
> **Jail:** 55 Minutes  
> **Points:** 0

## What the Law Means

Applies when a person knowingly possesses or controls a firearm or weapon that is prohibited within the State, including any weapon that cannot be lawfully purchased through a state-licensed gun store, firearms dealer, or authorized armory, any firearm capable of or modified for burst or fully automatic fire, any firearm lacking a required manufacturer serial number, any firearm with an altered, removed, scratched-off, obliterated, or defaced serial number, or any firearm that has been unlawfully modified or equipped with prohibited components. Possession may be actual, such as having the weapon on the person's body or in their immediate physical control, or constructive when the person knows the weapon is present and has the ability and intent to exercise control over it.

## When NOT to Charge

Do not apply this charge when a required element is missing. Suspicion, association, or conduct that merely resembles the offense is not enough by itself.

## What to Document

- Exact conduct observed or established
- Location and circumstances
- Facts establishing intent, knowledge, recklessness, or status when required
- Relevant victim, property, vehicle, weapon, substance, value, quantity, license, permit, sign, signal, or roadway condition
- Supporting statements, witnesses, records, photographs, video, or physical evidence

### Additional Enforcement Notes

LEO applicability: establish that the civilian knowingly possessed or exercised control over the weapon and identify the specific reason the weapon is considered illegal. Document the weapon type, make or model when known, serial number or the absence or condition of the serial number, any automatic-fire capability or prohibited modification, where the weapon was recovered, and facts connecting the civilian to it. If the weapon is considered illegal because it cannot be purchased through a licensed dealer or authorized armory, verify that status when practical. When the weapon is recovered from a shared vehicle, residence, room, container, or other area accessible to multiple people, mere proximity is not enough; document facts showing the civilian knew the weapon was present and exercised or intended to exercise control over it.
