# O.C.G.A. § 16-9-152 — Computer or Network Disruption

## Charge Information

> **Felony**  
> **Class:** Class B  
> **Fine:** $30,000  
> **Jail:** 40 Minutes  
> **Points:** 0

## What the Law Means

Knowingly introduces, transmits, or uses code or commands to disrupt, damage, impair, or deny authorized computer/network service. The offense requires the listed unlawful interference with, entry onto, taking of, or damage to property and any intent or value threshold stated by the charge.

## What You Need to Charge

Establish the conduct described by the offense and every required fact that makes the conduct unlawful. Document identity, location, intent or knowledge when required, and any relevant victim, property, vehicle, weapon, substance, value, quantity, license, permit, prior conviction, official order, sign, signal, or aggravating circumstance. Specifically establish: Knowingly introduces, transmits, or uses code or commands to disrupt, damage, impair, or deny authorized computer/network service. The offense requires the listed unlawful interference with, entry onto, taking of, or damage to property and any intent or value threshold stated by the charge.

## When NOT to Charge

Do not apply this charge when a required element is missing. Suspicion, association, or conduct that merely resembles the offense is not enough by itself.

## What to Document

- Exact conduct observed or established
- Location and circumstances
- Facts establishing intent, knowledge, recklessness, or status when required
- Relevant victim, property, vehicle, weapon, substance, value, quantity, license, permit, sign, signal, or roadway condition
- Supporting statements, witnesses, records, photographs, video, or physical evidence

### Additional Enforcement Notes

LEO applicability: establish ownership or lawful possession, lack of consent/authority, what property was taken/damaged/entered, the suspect's conduct and intent, and the value of loss or damage when grading depends on value. Photograph damage and identify the owner/victim when practical.
